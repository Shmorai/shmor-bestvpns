VPN free trials, renewals and cancelling: what to check before you pay

Long-term VPN plans and free trials can be good value, but they often renew automatically. A few checks before you enter your card details help you avoid paying for months you do not use.

By the BestVPNs editorial team · 18 November 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: FlyD on Unsplash

On this page

  1. Free trials usually need a card
  2. Auto-renewals: read the renewal terms
  3. Your cancellation and refund rights
  4. A simple checkout checklist
  5. How to cancel cleanly

Free trials usually need a card

The US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) warns that free trials often require a card number, so you can be charged if you do not cancel before the trial ends. Put the end date in your calendar. Before signing up, check the trial length, what you are agreeing to, how to cancel, and whether a pre-ticked box authorises later billing. Our guide to free VPNs covers alternatives to trials.

Auto-renewals: read the renewal terms

The FTC describes auto-renewals as plans that continue unless you actively cancel. Many VPN offers are priced for the first term only, and the renewal price or length may differ. On the checkout page, note the renewal date, the renewal price and the cancellation method. Our VPN checklist explains what else to compare, and you can see features explained before you pay for any.

Your cancellation and refund rights

In the US, the FTC says businesses must give you a simple way to cancel automatic billing, ideally explained before you pay. If cancelling fails, dispute the charge with your card issuer, keep records, and report problems at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and to your state attorney general.

In the UK, the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 give a general 14-day cancellation right for most online (distance) contracts. The government has also announced new subscription rules, with reminders before a trial ends or a 12-month-plus contract renews, easier online cancellation and a 14-day cooling-off period after renewal, which it expected to come into force in Spring 2027 (government announcement, April 2026). Until they apply, rely on the current law and each provider’s terms.

Many providers also advertise a money-back guarantee, which is their own policy rather than a legal right. Read the terms on the provider’s site, for example NordVPN’s plan and refund terms.

A simple checkout checklist

  • Screenshot the price, renewal price and renewal date.
  • Check whether the refund window is a legal right or the provider’s own promise.
  • Choose a term you will realistically use; shorter plans cost more per month but renew sooner.
  • Use a card or payment method you can dispute.
  • Set a reminder a week before renewal.

How to cancel cleanly

Cancel in your account settings if the option exists, and save the confirmation email. If you bought through an app store, cancel in your Apple or Google subscription settings, not only in the VPN app. If you also use a password manager or antivirus bundle from the same company, check each renewal separately. The same habit helps with other security apps that offer free trials, such as the Keeper password manager: note the date the trial ends and cancel before then if you don’t want to pay.

Frequently asked questions

Will a VPN free trial charge me automatically?
It can. The FTC advises that trials often need a card number and you may be charged if you do not cancel in time. Check the terms and note the end date.
Can I get a refund on a VPN subscription?
It depends. UK law gives a general 14-day cancellation right for most online contracts, and many providers add their own money-back guarantee. Read the provider’s terms for exact conditions.
Do VPNs raise the price when the plan renews?
Some plans renew at a different price from the intro offer. The checkout page and terms should state the renewal price, so check them before you pay.
