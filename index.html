What a VPN does (and doesn’t do)

A VPN (virtual private network) creates an encrypted connection, often called a tunnel, between your device and a server run by the VPN provider. Websites and apps see the VPN server’s IP address instead of yours, and your internet provider or the owner of a Wi‑Fi network can see that you’re connected to a VPN, but not which sites you visit.

A VPN is not a magic privacy shield. It doesn’t stop tracking cookies, logged-in accounts or browser fingerprinting, and it moves trust from your internet provider to the VPN provider. That’s why no-logs policies and independent audits matter so much. New to all this? Start with what is a VPN.

How to choose a VPN

  • Privacy first: a clear no-logs policy that has been independently audited, and a company that is open about who owns it and where it is based.
  • Security features: modern protocols such as WireGuard, a reliable kill switch and DNS leak protection. See VPN features explained.
  • Your use case: streaming, travel, gaming or just safer public Wi‑Fi each favour different strengths.
  • Your devices: apps for iPhone and Android, Windows and Mac, and routers, smart TVs and Fire TV, plus enough simultaneous connections for your household.
  • Fair terms: the renewal price (not just the first-term discount), the refund window and how easy it is to cancel.

For VPN users in the US, UK and Europe

Using a VPN is legal in the US, the UK and EU countries, although breaking the law or a service’s terms through a VPN is not. Some countries restrict VPNs, so check before you travel: see is a VPN legal?

If you live in the EU, the EU’s portability rules let you use many of your paid streaming subscriptions while temporarily in another EU country, without a VPN. UK residents lost that right after Brexit, which is one reason UK travellers often ask about VPNs. Our streaming guide explains what’s allowed.

Frequently asked questions

Is BestVPNs a VPN provider?
No. BestVPNs is a free guide and comparison site. When you choose a VPN, you subscribe directly with the provider, under their terms and privacy policy.
Do I really need a VPN?
Not always. Most websites already use HTTPS encryption. A VPN is most useful on untrusted Wi‑Fi, when you want to hide your browsing from your internet provider, when travelling, or to reach your home services from abroad. See what is a VPN.
Does a VPN make me anonymous?
No. A VPN hides your IP address and encrypts traffic between you and the VPN server, but accounts you log in to, cookies and browser fingerprinting can still identify you, and the VPN provider itself can see your connection. For stronger anonymity, people use Tor. Read VPN vs Tor vs proxy.
