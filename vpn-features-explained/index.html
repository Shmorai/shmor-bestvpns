Features explained

VPN features explained in plain English

VPN apps are full of settings with technical names. Here’s what each one does, and which ones matter for you.

Photo: Jordan Harrison on Unsplash

The essentials

  • Encryption: scrambles your traffic between your device and the VPN server. Mainstream VPNs use strong, well-studied ciphers such as AES-256 or ChaCha20.
  • Kill switch: blocks internet access if the VPN disconnects, so nothing leaks over your normal connection. Some apps offer an app-level kill switch that closes chosen apps instead.
  • DNS leak protection: makes sure your domain lookups go through the VPN rather than your internet provider.
  • Auto-connect: connects whenever you join an untrusted network.

Protocols

ProtocolWhat to know
WireGuardModern, fast and lean. Widely supported; some providers build their own protocols on it.
OpenVPNLong-established and open source. Runs over UDP (faster) or TCP (better on restrictive networks).
IKEv2/IPsecReconnects well when you switch between Wi‑Fi and mobile data. Built into many operating systems.
ProprietaryProvider-specific protocols. Look for independent audits.
PPTP / L2TPOlder. PPTP is considered insecure; avoid it.

Useful extras

  • Split tunnelling: choose which apps or sites use the VPN. Handy for local printers, banking apps or games.
  • Multi-hop (double VPN): routes traffic through two VPN servers. More privacy, but slower.
  • Obfuscation (stealth mode): disguises VPN traffic as ordinary web traffic, for networks that block VPNs.
  • Dedicated IP: an IP address only you use, which can reduce CAPTCHAs and help with allow-listed work systems. It’s less private than a shared IP.
  • Port forwarding: lets incoming connections reach your device, useful for some hosting or peer-to-peer uses. It’s offered by only some providers.
  • Tracker and ad blocking: DNS-level blocking of known ad, tracker and malware domains.

Frequently asked questions

Should I always turn on the kill switch?
For privacy, yes. It prevents your real IP address from being exposed if the VPN drops. If a particular app needs a direct connection, use split tunnelling rather than turning the kill switch off.
Which VPN protocol is best?
For most people, WireGuard (or a provider’s protocol based on it) offers the best balance of speed and security. OpenVPN over TCP is a good fallback on networks that block VPNs.
Is split tunnelling safe?
Yes, if you understand that apps you exclude don’t get the VPN’s protection. Exclude only apps that need it, such as local network tools.
