VPNs for Windows, Mac and Linux

On a computer you can use your provider’s app, the system’s built-in VPN settings or a browser extension. Here’s the difference, and how to make sure everything is really protected.

Photo: Caspar Camille Rubin on Unsplash

App, built-in settings or browser extension?

  • Provider app (recommended): the easiest way to get a kill switch, leak protection, split tunnelling and automatic updates.
  • Built-in VPN settings: Windows and macOS can connect to some VPN types (such as IKEv2) without an app, using details from your provider. Useful for work VPNs, but you may lose app-only features.
  • Browser extension: usually protects only that browser’s traffic, not your other apps. Some providers’ extensions act as a proxy rather than a full VPN.

Settings worth turning on

  • Kill switch (sometimes called “network lock”).
  • Launch and connect on start-up.
  • DNS leak protection, and IPv6 leak protection if your connection uses IPv6.
  • Split tunnelling for apps that don’t work well over the VPN, such as some banking or work tools.

Then run a leak test to check.

Common problems and fixes

  • Won’t connect: try another server or protocol, check your firewall or antivirus, and update the app.
  • Slow speeds: try a closer server and WireGuard; compare with a speed test without the VPN.
  • Sites blocking you: some sites block VPN IP addresses; try another server or split tunnelling.
  • Work laptop: follow your employer’s IT policy. Installing another VPN may conflict with the company VPN.

Last updated: 13 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

Do Windows and Mac have a built-in VPN?
They have built-in VPN clients that can connect to a VPN server, but not a VPN service. You still need a provider (or your employer’s VPN) to connect to.
Is a VPN browser extension enough?
Usually not for full protection: most extensions only cover that browser. Use the full app if you want every app on your computer protected.
Can I share my laptop’s VPN with a games console?
Yes. Windows and macOS can share an internet connection over Ethernet or Wi‑Fi, so a console can use the laptop’s VPN connection. A VPN router is a simpler long-term option.
