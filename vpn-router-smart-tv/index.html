VPNs on routers, smart TVs and Fire TV

Phones and laptops are easy. TVs, streaming sticks and consoles take a little more thought. Here are your options, from a simple app to protecting your whole home at the router.

Photo: Misha Feshchak on Unsplash

Devices that can run a VPN app

  • Amazon Fire TV and Fire TV Stick: many providers offer apps in the Amazon Appstore.
  • Android TV and Google TV: many providers offer apps on Google Play.
  • Apple TV: recent versions of tvOS support VPN apps, and some providers offer them.

Check your provider’s list of supported devices before you subscribe.

Devices without VPN apps

Many smart TVs, Roku players and games consoles can’t run VPN apps. Your options are:

  • A VPN on your router: protects every device on your network, including smart home gadgets.
  • A shared connection from a computer running the VPN.
  • Smart DNS: some providers offer it for streaming devices. It changes how domain names are looked up but doesn’t encrypt your traffic or hide your IP address, so it isn’t a privacy tool.

Router VPN: pros and cons

ProsCons
Covers every device, including ones without appsNot every router supports VPN client mode; you may need a compatible or pre-configured router
Counts as one connection on most plansRouter processors can limit VPN speed
Always on, nothing to rememberChanging server or turning it off per device is harder

Many routers with OpenVPN or WireGuard client support, or open firmware, can be set up using configuration files from your provider. Some providers sell or partner with pre-configured routers.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put a VPN on my Fire TV Stick?
Yes, many VPN providers offer apps for Fire TV devices in the Amazon Appstore. Install it, sign in and connect before opening your streaming app.
Does a VPN router slow down my internet?
It can, because the router does the encryption and many home routers have modest processors. Routers with faster processors and WireGuard support usually perform better.
Is smart DNS the same as a VPN?
No. Smart DNS doesn’t encrypt your traffic or hide your IP address. It may help some streaming apps but offers no privacy protection.
